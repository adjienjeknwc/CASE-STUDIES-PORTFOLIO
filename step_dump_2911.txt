# Designing MakeMyTrip's First-Time Flyer Experience for Tier-2/3 India

**Type:** 0-to-1 Product Case Study
**Role:** Product Manager (Case Study)
**Focus:** New user segment — first-time domestic flyers from Tier-2/3 India

---

## 1. The Opportunity

India's domestic aviation market has expanded rapidly beyond the eight metro cities, driven by the UDAN regional connectivity scheme and rising middle-class incomes in Tier-2/3 cities. A large share of new flyers today are booking their *first-ever flight* — not upgrading from an old booking app, but crossing from "flights are not for me" to "I can afford this."

MakeMyTrip, like most travel platforms, is built around a user who already knows how flying works: what a PNR is, what a layover means, how airport check-in and security work, what "hand baggage only" implies. That's a reasonable assumption for a Mumbai frequent flyer. It's a bad assumption for someone in Bareilly booking their first flight to see a relative in Bangalore.

**The question I'm solving:** *How might MakeMyTrip design a 0-to-1 experience specifically for first-time flyers from Tier-2/3 India — one that builds trust and removes confusion at every step, without making experienced flyers feel like the app is being "dumbed down"?*

I'm choosing this over a more generic 0-to-1 idea because it's a real, sizable, currently underserved segment — not a hypothetical persona invented for the case study.

---

## 2. Why This Segment, Why Now

- Regional airports and UDAN-linked routes have meaningfully increased flight availability from smaller cities, expanding the pool of people for whom flying is newly viable.
- Vernacular-first apps in other categories (Meesho in e-commerce, several fintech apps) have proven that Tier-2/3 users adopt digital products fast when trust and language barriers are removed — this is a validated pattern, not a bet.

## 8. Risks & Open Questions

- **Risk:** Auto-detecting "first-time flyer" purely from booking history could misfire (e.g., someone who's flown before but never booked on MakeMyTrip). Mitigation: make it easy to opt out with one tap, and don't gate any core functionality behind it.
- **Risk:** Regional-language content requires real translation quality, not literal machine translation — a bad translation could damage trust faster than no translation at all. Mitigation: start with 2-3 highest-volume regional languages and validate quality with native speakers before scaling.
- **Open question:** Is airport-day anxiety (H2) really the biggest driver, or would research reveal payment trust (H3) is bigger than I'm assuming? This case study leads with H2 based on reasoning about where competitors have already invested — in a real role, I'd want the support-ticket and funnel data before committing engineering time.

---

## 9. What I'd Do Next If This Were Real

1. Pull first-time vs. repeat flyer funnel data and regional support ticket themes before building anything
2. Run 5-10 interviews in at least two Tier-2/3 cities, in-language, to sanity-check which anxiety point is actually biggest
3. Ship the countdown checklist and plain-language fare rules as a fast A/B test — both are low-cost enough to validate within a few weeks
4. Only invest in video content and helpline staffing once the cheap version shows a measurable lift in booking-to-boarding completion
