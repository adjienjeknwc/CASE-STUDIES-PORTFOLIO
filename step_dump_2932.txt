## 1. Executive Summary

Dermalink turns a selfie into a structured skincare diagnosis: it maps the face into three zones, identifies concerns per zone, and generates a morning/night routine priced across three budget tiers — all without a dermatologist visit or a store aisle of guesswork. The product sits at the intersection of two fast-growing trends: **AI-driven visual diagnostics** and **personalized beauty commerce**.

What makes this case study interesting from a PM/BA angle isn't just the feature set — it's the set of **trust, privacy, and regulatory questions** a product like this has to answer before it can scale, which I've broken out in detail below because they're often the difference between a demo and a defensible product.

## 15. Key Takeaways

- Dermalink sits in a large, fast-growing market, but its real differentiation is **positioning**, not just the AI feature — a brand-agnostic diagnostic tool is a different (and less crowded) business than a DTC skincare subscription.
- The **client-side privacy redaction** is genuinely strong product thinking on the developer's part — I called it out explicitly because it's the kind of detail that separates "AI demo" from "product I'd trust with my face."
- The two things I'd fix before any public launch aren't features — they're **trust infrastructure**: securing the API key server-side, and tightening the health-claims language before "clinical-grade" becomes a liability instead of a selling point.
- I approached this the way I would any existing build handed to me: understand the regulatory and trust surface first, then design the roadmap — because for a product touching biometric and health-adjacent data, that surface *is* the product risk.
